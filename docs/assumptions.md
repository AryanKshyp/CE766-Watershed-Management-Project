# Assumptions for the final Subarnarekha comparison

## Record and reference case

- Daily rainfall and GLEAM potential evapotranspiration (PET) are catchment means. Discharge is measured at the outlet. GLEAM actual evapotranspiration (AET) is an independent model-based estimate. The two GHI drainage areas are 12,785.0 km² (06008) and 6,350.2 km² (06001). The outlet tree fraction is 0.307405. [CAMELS-IND v2.2](https://zenodo.org/records/14999580)
- The fitted GR4J parameters, bounds, objective and seed (42) are in the calibration script and tables. Initial production and routing storage are each half their respective capacity. Run continuously from January 1980 to December 2018. Score daily calibration in 1981–2009 and validation in 2010–2018 on available observations. [GR4J](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022169403002257)
- For planning, take whole June–May years after warm-up, June 1981 to May 2018. Keep 35 of 37 years with at least 80% observed days (the preliminary completeness rule). Years starting in 2015 and 2016 fail. The 35 retained years happen to have observations on every day. Annual yield uses mean daily runoff on common days × 365 × area / 1,000, in MCM. This standardises leap years as in the preliminary calculation. Dependable yield is the 25th percentile, with linear interpolation.
- “Deficient” means the 12 years in the bottom third of June–September rainfall among those 35, at or below 970.393 mm. It is a local historical class. The scenario outputs are continuous simulations evaluated over the same years. Missing discharge stays missing throughout.
- The preliminary numbers used a series whose January–May 1980 fragment could count as a water year. The final series also drops the warm-up year and extends through the last whole year ending May 2018. The comparable 75% dependable yields are 4,596.8 and 5,596.8 MCM (observed and simulated).
- Annual P minus Q is a water-balance residual that also includes exchanges and storage changes. Compare on common retained days with GLEAM AET and a Zhang-based screen using P and PET annualised on those days. The January–May regulation check subtracts same-day Kharkai discharge from the Jamshedpur outlet, retaining seasons with ≥80% paired coverage. The difference includes intervening runoff, routing, use and gauge error. The final record ends in 2018.

## Siting and water balance

All hypothetical investments are above Chandil. This isolates one location where all four levers could affect the same reservoir. The rest of the 12,785 km² watershed stays in the calculation. Each lever is either absent or present at one stated scale. Evaluating all 16 combinations exhausts this small choice set. There is no measured adoption rate, mapped area of eligible upland, district budget ceiling or continuous optimum.

| Lever | Size and behaviour when selected | Basis |
|---|---|---|

Multiply GR4J PET by the ratio of estimated annual ET at new and original cover. This is a sensitivity approximation to the annual relationship. [Zhang, Dawes & Walker (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1029/2000WR900325) |

The two surface buckets start empty and are aggregated by structure type. At each daily step, spill above capacity, evaporate at GLEAM PET times wetted area, seep at the scenario rate times wetted area, then withdraw for irrigation. Wetted area scales as the two-thirds power of the fraction filled (similar-shape approximation). Seepage returns to the routing store the same day for ponds and the next day for check dams. Check-dam inflow comes after upland routing, so upstream water is deducted before downstream capture. The bucket receives runoff as its rainfall contribution. Open-water losses are additional to the baseline land-surface model.

Farmers can draw up to one nominal storage volume per October–February season, spread evenly over its calendar days. This common operating rule limits the command area and avoids assuming repeated refills can all be pumped. Equivalent hectares = seasonal withdrawal (MCM) × 100,000 / seasonal need (mm). Assume water reaches fields without further losses. Pumping access, conveyance, crop mix, soil water saved by bunds, well irrigation and forest co-benefits are outside this measure. Zero additional hectares under the baseline says nothing about existing farming.

## Chandil and downstream users

- Catchment 5,646 km², full-reservoir storage 1,446.966 MCM and dead storage 58.622 MCM yield 1,388.344 MCM active storage. Start half-full before warm-up. The smaller stress capacity represents operating restrictions. Calculate evaporation as daily PET times estimated water area. Use 173.4664 km² submergence as an approximate full-area bound and scale with gross volume to the power 2/3. A surveyed area–volume curve would improve this. [JREDA tender, Schedule B, p. 19](https://api.jreda.com/all-uploaded-img/Tender/644ce06965632.NIBDocumentNIB04.pdf) and [CWC reservoir particulars](https://cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/reply_173.pdf)
- Approximate inflow as GR4J depth from 06008 parameters and forcings × Chandil area. The fit is to a regulated downstream gauge, so this gives an inflow proxy with uncertain timing. Getalsud operations remain implicit. Downstream add the 6,350.2 km² Kharkai simulated with its own forcings and parameters and the remaining 788.8 km² with 06008's runoff depth. This avoids routing the Kharkai through Chandil. Travel time and intake capacity are omitted.
- The project design assigns 494 MCM from Chandil to industrial and drinking use in Jharkhand. Spread this over 365 days (1.3534 MCM/day) as a constant allocation proxy for the city objective. This is a design-scale scenario, not a measurement of Jamshedpur demand. After evaporation, allocate water to this demand, then to a fixed river target; spill excess. Remove delivered city water once, as fully abstracted. Omit canal irrigation, other priority releases, direct reservoir rainfall as a separate term, and return flows. [CWC project description](https://cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/nwauser/modulevolume.pdf)
- Use observed all-season Q95 (5th percentile on retained observed days, 3.051 m³/s) as the screening threshold below the city. The reference Chandil release target is its area share, 5,646/12,785 × Q95. Tributaries and spill supply the balance. This simple operating rule is assumed, not the utility's actual rule. Test the threshold on January–May days. River habitat requirements could differ. Supply reliability counts days meeting the full assumed demand over each selected set of water years.

## Stress case and money

The combined stress run uses the same measured rainfall sequence, 10% higher PET, 25% higher demand, half the live capacity and half the controlled river release target. Increase pond and check-dam seepage from 2 to 5 mm/day over wetted area and seasonal water need from 375 to 450 mm. The crop depths span a middle and upper case within the project's 300–450 mm planning range. The seepage pair tests sensitivity to faster drainage. The other multipliers are rounded contrasts to explore hotter weather, more demand and restricted operation. They have no assigned forecast probability. All four lever choices are evaluated under both sets of assumptions. The final course comparison does not estimate separate effects of the stresses or sample parameter distributions.

Capital cost in crore = sum of unit rate × quantity / 10,000,000. Apply 0.7 and 1.3 to estimate a cost range, following the project's ±30% uncertainty. The references cover different years and schemes, with no inflation adjustment, maintenance, energy, land or discounting. Treat the figures as indicative outlays in source prices. A cost is affordable only in relation to an actual budget.
